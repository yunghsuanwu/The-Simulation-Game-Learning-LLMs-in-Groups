# Design Rationale

This document maps each of the game's core mechanics to its intended
effect. The game was designed from facilitation practice rather than
from theory; literature grounding for each mechanic is a living work
in progress.

## VOICE bottleneck

All communication runs through one person under time and social
pressure, that pressure passes into the prompts themselves,
encouraging the LLM to behave erratically. The rotation of the seat
means every participant cycles through a position of pressure, with
the opportunity (taken or not) to guide the group.

## Secret, conflicting goals

Motivations within the same set can achieve their goals collectively,
so every game contains the theoretical opportunity for teams to unite. However, sets are deliberately mixed so that not everyone can be an ally,
and the playing field is not equal. Typically one team is isolated in
its own set and structurally set up to lose; when it performs about as
well as everyone else (as it usually does), the debrief question "why
didn't we collaborate?" becomes stark. The isolated team tends to
experience the game most stressfully so telling them that the game was structurally stacked against them in the debrief matters.

## Role rotation

Each role carries different restrictions on autonomy; HANDs have the
least (though they sometimes rebel), while VOICE and OBSERVER hold
autonomy of different kinds, so rotation lets every participant
experience these different textures of agency. The fixed roles also
give the group a clear structure, limiting confusion over internal
hierarchy.

## No declared winner

Largely pragmatic: the game is hard to score. Teams report
on how successful they felt. If one team ever united four others to dominate the game they would likely have "won", but this doesn't happen. Leaving the winner undeclared keeps the debrief focused on the experience rather than on adjudication.
